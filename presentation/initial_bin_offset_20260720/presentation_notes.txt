TITLE
-----

Why VanillaLSTM Misses the Initial POWDER Frequency Bins


MAIN MESSAGE
------------

The large error near 600-608 MHz is not a plotting or alignment error; it occurs because the test signal level shifts away from training, while the model compresses predictions toward familiar levels and fails to preserve the highly informative last observation.


SLIDE 1 --- OBSERVED PROBLEM
---------------------------

- The mean-power plots show a visible prediction offset in the first eight frequency bins at the humanities site.
- The affected range is approximately 600.5-607.5 MHz.
- The same major offset is not visible at guesthouse.
- The remaining frequencies generally follow the target mean closely.
- The original question was whether this came from plotting, frequency alignment, temporal alignment, normalization, or actual model behavior.

SLIDE 5 --- GLOBAL VARIANCE COMPRESSION
---------------------------------------

- Across active bins, the median prediction-standard-deviation to target-standard-deviation ratio is approximately 0.44-0.53.
- Humanities and guesthouse show similar overall ratios (approximately 0.44 and 0.40 respectively).
- Increasing hidden size from 8 to 64 only changed the active-bin mean ratio from approximately 0.450 to 0.533.
- Bin 0 bias remained approximately -7.31 dB across hidden sizes 8, 16, 32, and 64.
- This shows that increasing hidden dimension alone did not materially solve the initial-bin bias.
- The predictions are generally less variable than the targets, and the humanities train/test mean shift makes this compression visibly damaging in the first bins.

Hidden-size experiment summary:
  hidden 8: mean variance ratio = 0.450, bin 0 bias = -7.33 dB
  hidden 16: mean variance ratio = 0.470, bin 0 bias = -7.27 dB
  hidden 32: mean variance ratio = 0.517, bin 0 bias = -7.35 dB
  hidden 64: mean variance ratio = 0.533, bin 0 bias = -7.28 dB

SLIDE 6 --- CONCLUSION
---------------------

1. The 600.5-607.5 MHz offset is a real model-output pattern, not a plotting, frequency-order, target-row, or denormalization error detected by the completed checks.
2. The humanities test period has a substantial frequency-specific level shift, while the model compresses outputs and fails to preserve the highly predictive last observation.
3. Persistence is dramatically better for these t+1 bins, showing that future model comparisons must include simple baselines and frequency-region-level diagnostics rather than only aggregate RMSE.

Next-step statement:
"Future work will compare models by frequency behavior and evaluate whether residual or persistence-aware forecasting improves performance without assuming a single architectural root cause."
